# Europe: VJ plan

> A slow summer stroll through the cities you've been to. Each street runs straight toward the city's most famous building. Every 32 beats the walk reaches a corner and turns 90° left or right at random, and the street around the corner is in the next city.

**Song:** *Mat Kearney & AFSHeeN, Better Than I Used To Be* (the mp3 in this folder)
**File:** `europe/europe.html` (replaces the current placeholder), opened from the VJ menu in `index.html`

---

## 1. Your brief → the design

| You asked for | How the plan does it |
|---|---|
| The vibe and "aura" of a Europe trip | Warm golden-hour to blue-hour light, soft haze, film grain and light leaks. A passport stamp with the city's name on every arrival, and a passport page of all the stamps when the song ends |
| A slow walk forward | Eye-level camera (1.6 m), about 1.2–1.5 m/s, a soft footstep on every beat, a little hand-held sway |
| Colourful, historic, narrow houses | A procedural street kit: narrow plots (4–9 m wide), 2–7 floors, every facade painted in its city's style |
| About 32 beats, then a 90° turn left or right at random | Turns are timed from the song's beats and land on the first beat of a musical phrase. Left or right is a coin flip |
| Each turn switches city | The street around the corner is built in the next city's style while you walk. Sky, light and colour cross-fade during the turn |
| A long street with the main building in front | Each street points straight at its landmark 200–300 m away, so it is in view the whole time |
| 8 or 16 cities, including yours | Your 7 + Paris + Berlin = 9, so one play visits each city exactly once. More can be added later (§4) |
| The vibe of each city, with details | Per-city houses, light, props and one "signature" beat effect (§3) |

## 2. The trip at a glance

```
                        ▲  landmark of city A (a view, never reached)
                        │
                        │  street A (city A)
                        │
   ─────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────▶  landmark of city B
                        │↱  street B (city B)
                        │   the turn: one bar ≈ 2.5 s, ends on beat 1
                        │
                        ●  you, walking up street A
```

Left turns are the mirror image. The houses on the corner you walk around are "two-faced": painted as city A on the side facing street A, and as city B on the side facing street B.

One play of the song (3:15) is about **9 streets**: the first city plus 8 turns. With 9 cities in shuffled order, one play visits every city exactly once.

### Per-city details

**Warsaw**
- Houses: 3–5-floor tenements in saturated pastels (salmon, mustard, mint, ochre), painted bands and sgraffito, steep tiled roofs, arched doorways.
- Details: wall lanterns on iron brackets, flower boxes, café umbrellas, a little mermaid (Syrenka) sign, pigeons on the square.
- On the beat: pigeons lift off on big hits; the castle clock face glows on the "1".

**Budapest**
- Houses: tall eclectic and Art Nouveau blocks (cream, ochre, grey-green), ornate balconies, tall windows, carved doorways.
- Details: tram rails and overhead wires, a yellow tram gliding across, iron lamp posts, a courtyard glimpse with ruin-bar fairy lights.
- On the beat: the Parliament's floodlights breathe with the bass; small sparks on the tram wire on snares.

**Vienna**
- Houses: baroque and Biedermeier facades (cream, Schönbrunn yellow, pale pink), pilasters, window pediments, ground-floor shops with gilded lettering.
- Details: a Fiaker horse carriage, café terraces, Viennese lanterns, a plague-column-style monument halfway down.
- On the beat: the cathedral's tiled roof glints on hi-hats; a "ring" of light from the spire at each new phrase.

**Rome**
- Houses: narrow plaster houses in ochre, terracotta, burnt orange and rose; green shutters, ivy and bougainvillea, small balconies with pots, laundry lines between windows.
- Details: Vespas, a nasone drinking fountain, trattoria tables with checked cloths, string lights, sampietrini cobbles.
- On the beat: laundry and string lights sway with the bass.

**Cluj-Napoca**
- Houses: 2–3-floor Austro-Hungarian facades in lemon, peach, sage and lilac; baroque window frames, arched carriage gates.
- Details: café terraces, trees, bicycles, posters (a nod to the Untold festival).
- On the beat: fairy lights over the street pulse; on drops, a soft sweep of festival light behind the church.

**Istanbul**
- Houses: 2–4-floor houses in strong colours (red, yellow, teal, violet) with overhanging bay windows (cumba), on a steep cobbled lane.
- Details: hanging kilims, mosaic lanterns, cats on the steps, a simit cart, tea glasses on small tables, gulls over the domes.
- On the beat: mosaic lanterns pulse in colour; gulls circle the minarets at each phrase.

**Ohrid**
- Houses: white stone ground floors with dark timber upper floors hanging over the lane, wooden balconies, terracotta roofs.
- Details: fig trees, stone steps, the lake opening up at the end, with boats, swallows, and mountains across the water.
- On the beat: the lake sparkles; swallows swoop at each phrase.

**Paris**
- Houses: cream limestone Haussmann blocks, wrought-iron balconies on the 2nd and 5th floors, zinc mansard roofs with dormers and chimney pots.
- Details: café terraces with red awnings and bistro chairs, a Morris column, a Wallace fountain.
- On the beat: café lights pulse; the tower does its famous white *sparkle* on the biggest drop.

**Berlin**
- Houses: tall Gründerzeit apartment blocks in sand, sage, salmon and grey-teal; tall windows in stucco surrounds, a stack of small iron balconies up the middle of each house, shops and a carriage gate below.
- Details: linden trees along both kerbs, round poster columns (Litfaßsäulen), bicycles against the walls, gas lanterns, German shop signs (Späti, Bäckerei, Kneipe, Imbiss…).
- The landmark: the Brandenburg Gate with its six columns and the Quadriga on top. Through the passages you see the Tiergarten, and a small golden Victory Column far down the axis.

## 5. The walk and the turn

**Walking.**
- The camera is at eye level (1.6 m). It moves at a steady 1.2–1.5 m/s: a bit faster when the music is loud, slower when it's quiet.
- Every beat is a footstep, with a small bob and sway like Twilight's walk but gentler.
- You can look around a little with the mouse, as in Twilight.
- One 32-beat phrase covers about 25–30 m, so the landmark is a slowly growing view. A gentle telephoto drift (the view narrows a few degrees over the phrase) makes it loom.

**The street.**
- Each city gets one straight street, 6–20 m wide depending on the city: narrow in Rome and Istanbul, wide in Paris and Budapest.
- Side streets open every 40–60 m, so the corner you turn at doesn't look special.
- The street ends in a square with the landmark. It's scaled and placed to fill about a fifth to a quarter of the screen height when the walk starts.

**The turn.**
1. The corner is placed exactly where the walk will be one bar before the phrase ends. The walking speed is planned ahead, so this distance is known when the street is built.
2. During that last bar (≈2.5 s), the camera curves around the corner on a tight quarter circle (≈2 m radius). The head turns a little ahead of the body, so you look into the new street just before you face it.
3. The turn finishes on beat 1 of the next phrase: the new landmark is straight ahead and the passport stamp lands.
4. Left or right is random.

**Swapping cities at the corner.**
- While you walk one street, the next city's street is built in small slices of a few milliseconds per frame, so nothing stutters.
- The corner houses are two-faced (§2). Real corners often join two quite different buildings, and the turn is quick.
- During the turn, the sky, sun, fog colour and colour grade cross-fade from city A to city B. A soft warm flare in the middle of the turn hides the moment of change.
- After the turn, street A is thrown away once it's behind you, and the world is re-centred so coordinates stay small.

**City order.** Shuffled: every city once before any repeats, and never the same city twice in a row. The first city is random each play. For testing: `europe.html?city=roma`, and a city lock in the owner panel.

**The end.** The last street is built shorter. In the final phrase you walk out into the square, and the camera slows and tilts up to the landmark as the music ends. Then the passport page of stamps fades in.

## 6. Music

### The song (analysed)

- 3:15, steady **~96 BPM** (308 beats). One bar ≈ 2.5 s, so **32 beats ≈ 20 s**.
- A quiet intro until ~0:21, then sections that build to two big drops, each after a 3-beat pause.
- One stretch (about 1:21–2:12) doesn't fit a plain 32-beat pattern: the bar count drifts by about a beat. So turns can't simply be counted from the start; they re-sync at each section change.

| Time | In the music | In the VJ |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | quiet intro | first city, its stamp, slow walk |
| ~0:21 | the beat comes in | turn 1 |
| ~0:41 | | turn 2 |
| ~1:01 | big rise | turn 3 lands on it |
| ~1:19 | 3-beat pause | the walk stops, the lights hold their breath |
| ~1:21 | **drop** | turn 4 lands on the drop, burst of light |
| ~1:41 | | turn 5 |
| ~2:00–2:12 | breakdown (quiet) | a longer, calmer walk (~49 beats) |
| ~2:12 | rise | turn 6 |
| ~2:30 | 3-beat pause | the walk stops |
| ~2:32 | **drop** | turn 7 + burst |
| ~2:52 | | turn 8 |
| ~3:13–3:15 | end | the finale and the passport page |

These times come from analysing the mp3. The exact beats get checked in M1 with an on-screen timeline view.

### How turn times are chosen (any song)

1. Find the beats (tempo and phase), and estimate which beat is the "1" of each bar.
2. Find section changes: jumps in loudness and in harmony, snapped to the beats.
3. Pick turn points: normally every 32 beats, but a turn moves to a nearby section change when there is one. The walk stretches or shrinks (roughly 24–50 beats) so the turn lands on the music.
4. Fast songs (above ~130 BPM) count in half-time (64 beats), so cities don't fly past.

### What else moves with the music

| In the music | In the picture |
|---|---|
| Every beat | a footstep |
| Kick / bass | street lamps and lit windows pulse; the camera eases forward slightly |
| Hi-hats | small glints: roof tiles, water, sparkles |
| Loud sections | slightly faster walk, richer colour, birds in the sky |
| The pause before a drop | walking stops, the light dims and holds |
| The drop | the turn lands, a burst of light, the city's signature effect |

### Where the beats come from (same idea as Twilight)

- **Opened over http (the website):** the page downloads and analyses the song when it opens.
- **Opened straight from disk (file://):** the browser won't let the page read the song file. So the default song's beats, section changes, turn times and a small loudness curve are worked out in advance and stored inside the page (a few KB), like Twilight's built-in beats.
- **Your own song** (owner panel, drag and drop): analysed from the file. This works from disk too.
- **Fallback:** live beat detection while the song plays.

## 7. How the streets are built

**Houses (the street kit)**
- Plots: narrow frontages (4–9 m), random but with each city's own mix. Now and then a wider palace or church. Small set-backs and height jumps keep the roofline lively.
- Facades are painted in code onto one texture sheet per city: wall colour, window types (arched, square, with pediments), shutters, balconies, cornices, heavy stone ground floors, shopfronts with awnings and signs. Each house gets its own tint, so no two match. The same painting also gives:
  - a relief map, so the walls catch the low sun;
  - a "lit windows" mask for the evening cities.
- Real 3D where it shows: cornices, roofs (gabled, hipped, mansard, flat with a parapet), dormers, chimneys, balconies, bay windows (Istanbul, Ohrid), awnings, flower boxes. Repeated pieces are drawn in batches, so a whole street costs only a few dozen draw calls.

**Ground:** cobbles in each city's pattern (Rome's fan-laid sampietrini, granite setts), pavements and kerbs, tram rails in Budapest, Vienna and Istanbul.

**Props:** lamps in each city's own design, café furniture, umbrellas, string lights, laundry, plants and trees, vehicles (Vespa, tram, carriage), fountains and statues, flocks of birds (pigeons, gulls, swallows).

**Landmarks: paper cut-outs.** Each landmark is painted in code as a few layers of coloured card, like a pop-up book. Every piece gets a darker cut edge, a soft shadow on the card behind it, and a light catch along its top edge, plus paper grain in the shader. The layers stand a few metres apart at the end of the street (for example the Brandenburg Gate: Tiergarten trees, the back colonnade, the guardhouses, the gate, the Quadriga). So they shift against each other as you walk, and you see real depth through the openings. They're seen from 200+ m away, so the **silhouette** comes first and detail second.
- A light mask painted alongside the colours lets windows glow and the stone take floodlight in the evening cities. Paler stone takes the light more gently than dark iron, so it stays golden instead of burning white. The Eiffel Tower's lattice also carries its sparkle.
- Each landmark is painted into one texture sheet (layers packed together), a few pieces per frame, then uploaded a strip per frame. Its canvases are reused city after city, so no big allocations cause garbage-collection pauses.
- Landmarks thin out the haze a little (62% of the street's fog), because they are the point of each view.

**Blocks and streaming.** You only walk ~25 m between corners, so the street you came from and the one after next run side by side, one block apart.
- **Block depth:** the rows of houses backing onto that block share its depth, so houses never overlap. Before this, two house fronts landing on the same wall flickered against each other.
- **Near and far parts:** each street is built as a near part (up to and around its next corner) and a far part (the rest of the long street, the square, the landmark). The near part shows from the turn two streets before; the far part only from the turn into the street until you turn out of it, because streets a corner or two apart cross each other out there.
- **Ohrid's lake:** it starts beyond the neighbouring city's square, so nothing stands in the water during a turn.

**Sky and air:** each city has its own sky (gradient, sun, clouds) and a distant skyline of rooftops and domes behind the landmark. Height fog glows warmer toward the sun. This haze carries most of the "aura".

**Light:** a low sun casts long shadows across the street; the shadow follows the camera and is switched off on Low quality. Soft sky fill, and glowing windows and lamps in the evening cities.

**Final image:** bloom (glow around bright light), film-like contrast, a colour grade per city, vignette, grain, faint colour fringing, and warm light leaks at turns. Sun rays down the street as an option.

## 8. The "trip" on screen

- **Passport stamp** on each arrival: "Wien" in big type, "Österreich · 48.21° N 16.37° E" small, slightly rotated and inked like a real stamp. It fades after ~4 s.
- **End of the trip:** a passport page with a stamp for every city in this play, plus "Walk again" and "Back to start" (like Twilight's end card).
- **Optional route map:** a tiny sketch of Europe in a corner that draws the dashed route from city to city at each turn.
- **Lyrics (optional):** Twilight's lyrics display can be reused if you add an `.lrc` file for this song.
- **Start:** "Click anywhere to begin", because browsers need one click before sound can play.
- **Owner panel** on the <kbd>`</kbd> key, like Twilight: walking pace, bar nudge (±1 beat), city lock, quality, mouse look, timeline view.

## 9. Tech

- **One file:** `europe/europe.html`, like Twilight: three.js 0.160 from jsDelivr, works both from disk and on the website, and loads `../vj-bridge.js` for the VJ menu.
- **Copied and adapted from Twilight:** random and noise helpers, settings storage, the audio engine and its clock, track loading and analysis, lyrics, the owner panel, the quality manager, post-processing, floating dust motes, the end card, test hooks. Twilight itself is not changed.
- **New parts:**
  - city table: palette, plot rules, facade painter, roofs, props, landmark builder, sky and light preset, stamp text;
  - street builder, which works in small slices;
  - landmark designs (paper cut-outs) and the card painter;
  - turn director: schedule and camera path;
  - atmosphere cross-fade;
  - stamp and passport display.
- **Budget:** 60 fps on a mid-range laptop at 1080p (High), 30+ fps on phones (Low). About ≤150 draw calls and ≤1.5 M triangles per frame. At most 2–3 cities' textures in memory: the current city and the next.
- **Reduced motion:** with the system setting on, there's no bob or flashes and turns are gentler.
- **Test hooks** (`window.__eu`): jump to a time, force a turn, show any city, render one frame. Used to screenshot every city.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Plan |
|---|---|
| Procedural houses look cheap | Spend M2 on one city until it convinces; compare side by side with reference photos |
| A landmark isn't recognisable from 200 m | Silhouette first; check each one as a small thumbnail |
| A stutter when the next street is built | Build in slices during the 20 s walk; paint textures one city ahead; upload big textures a strip per frame; reuse big buffers so the garbage collector stays quiet |
| Buildings of two streets overlap | Rows backing onto the same block share its depth; far parts only show while you look down that street |
| A visible seam at the corner | Two-faced corner houses + light cross-fade + mid-turn flare |
| The "1" of the bar is hard to find in this song | Re-sync at section changes; the owner panel can nudge the bar by a beat; check against the timeline view |
| Phones too slow | Low quality: no shadows, fewer props, lower resolution, simpler facades |
