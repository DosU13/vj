# Cleopatra: VJ plan

> An anima VJ. One song, one sunset, one encounter. A woman seen from behind at the edge of a lake, who never quite lets you look at her. Once, on the song's biggest moment, she turns.

**Title:** Cleopatra. It's only a name: nothing in the VJ is about Cleopatra.
**Song:** *Miles & Miles, She's So High* (the mp3 in this folder)
**File:** `cleopatra/cleopatra.html`, opened from the VJ menu in `index.html`.

---

## 1. The idea

A normal VJ shows more as the music gets louder. This one holds back. The spark is a projection, so the VJ builds the gap and the conditions for a spark, and the viewer's own mind fills her in: **build the gap, not the girl.**

| The anima | How the VJ does it |
|---|---|
| A projection, not a person | No face, no name. She's seen from behind (a *Rückenfigur*, like Friedrich's *Woman at a Window*), always backlit. Her best angle is the *profil perdu*, the "lost profile": head turned away, only the line of the cheek and the lashes |
| Mystery + beauty + a fleeting moment + unspoken emotion | Glimpses of a few seconds, slow motion, near-misses. No words on screen |
| Silence = mystery, music = fate | Counterpoint: she shows herself in the song's quiet dips, and the loud choruses play over her absence. The one exception is the turn (§3) |
| She can't be possessed | The Orpheus rule: look straight at her and she dissolves (§6) |
| A guide into the unconscious | She walks ahead and the camera follows her: from the warm shore, to the water's edge, into the night |
| Water, moon and night | A lake at sunset: reflections, mist, stars on still water, a young moon |
| The four stages | Eve, Helen, Mary and Sophia are the light of one sunset (§4) |
| Crystallization (Stendhal) | Each glimpse leaves more sparkle on her outline; after she's gone it falls like snow (§7) |
| Your own conclusion: the spark is a projection | The ending: the shore is empty, but she's standing in the lake's reflection |

**Your spark ingredients, and where they work**

| Ingredient | Where |
|---|---|
| Physical attraction (25%) | Posture and light: weight on one leg, the nape of the neck, hair lit from behind, a long dress in the wind. Never sharp and close at the same time |
| Uncertainty (15%) | Two almost-turns before the real one. Glimpses come at different moments and places each play |
| Childhood patterns (15%) | Summer-evening nostalgia: cicadas, a rope swing on a willow, pollen in the sunbeams, home-movie grain |
| Behaviour (15%) | She's absorbed in her own world: watching the water, unhurried, still while everything else moves |
| Context (15%) | Excitement transfer: in chorus 2 the camera lifts off and races low over the water, and the breakdown right after puts her in front of you while the rush is still in your body |
| Social proof (10%) | Flowers lean toward her, swallows circle above her, petals drift her way |
| Own state (5%) | It opens on solitude: a warm, empty, beautiful shore |

## 2. The song (analysed)

- 3:24, steady **~126 BPM** in 4/4 (420 beats). One bar ≈ 1.9 s, 8 bars ≈ 15 s.
- It's upbeat and sung, not the slow wordless piece I first suggested, but its shape suits the idea very well: loud choruses with clear dips between them, and one long breakdown before the last chorus.

| Time | In the music | |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:09 | intro, no bass | quiet |
| 0:09 | the beat and the bass come in: verse 1 | |
| 0:32–0:42 | pre-chorus: drums and bass pull back | dip |
| 0:42–1:08 | chorus 1 | loud |
| 1:08–1:12 | a 4-second break (the intro sound returns) | quiet |
| 1:12–1:26 | verse 2 | |
| 1:26–1:44 | pre-chorus 2 | dip |
| 1:44–2:12 | chorus 2 | loud |
| 2:12–2:26 | breakdown: no drums, quietest at ~2:24 | quiet |
| ~2:26 | the band and the voice come back in full | **the turn** |
| 2:41–3:09 | final chorus | loudest |
| 3:09–3:21 | outro, fading | quiet |

These times come from two analyses of the mp3: the Meadow's own analysis (beats, bars, entrances) and a section analysis with librosa. M1 checks them against an on-screen timeline.

**Where the turn goes.** The Meadow's entrance finder rates the 1:12 entrance highest, but that's only a third of the way in, and most of the song would be left for her absence. The turn goes on the ~2:26 entrance instead: the end of the song's longest held breath, 70% of the way through, right before the final chorus. For any other song, the director picks the entrance between 55% and 85% of the way through with the longest, deepest dip before it. The owner panel can nudge it by a beat or pick another entrance.

## 3. The arc

The sun's height and the four stages for each part are in §4.

| Phase | Song | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **0. Prelude** | ~8 s before the song | After the first click: only wind, water and cicadas, while the shore fades in out of a white-gold haze. An empty rope swing hangs from a willow |
| **1. Solitude** | 0:00–0:09 intro | The glide along the shore path, the lake to one side, no one there. Late-afternoon heat: shimmer over the grass, pollen in the sunbeams. The song sounds muffled, like a party across the water |
| **2. She passes** | 0:09 | On the beat's entrance a blur of hair, cream fabric and crimson crosses the frame, so close it's out of focus: under a second, with a rustle of fabric. The song opens up to full sound |
| | 0:09–0:32 verse 1 | The camera turns after her. She's walking away down the path, 15–30 m ahead, backlit, never looking back |
| | 0:32–0:42 pre-chorus | In the dip she stops at the water's edge and looks out over the lake. The first clear view of her, and the first few crystals |
| **3. Near-misses** | 0:42–1:08 chorus 1 | The camera turns to the lake, and the chorus plays over her absence: the sun's path on the water, gusts running across it as bands of dark ripples, swallows, petals. She shows only at the edges: once in a reflection, once far along the shore |
| | 1:08–1:12 break | In the hush she's suddenly there, closer, at the end of the jetty, and her head starts to turn (almost-turn 1). The music comes back at 1:12 with a gust of petals between you, and when it clears the jetty is empty |
| | 1:12–1:26 verse 2 | Searching. She's among the reeds, under the willow, in the water. With nobody touching the controls, the camera drifts onto her once and she dissolves (the Orpheus rule, shown once) |
| | 1:26–1:44 pre-chorus 2 | She stands on a rock above the shore, above eye level. Almost-turn 2 goes further: the ear, the line of the cheek. She turns back as chorus 2 hits |
| | 1:44–2:12 chorus 2 | The rush. The glide lifts over the reeds and runs fast and low over the water toward the huge low sun, swallows all around, then sinks back to the shore as the chorus ends |
| **4. The turn** | 2:12–2:26 breakdown | The camera settles 8–10 m from her, a little below her. She stands at the water's edge against the sun, which is touching the far hills. Slow motion: petals hang in the air, her scarf streams slowly, the crystals are at their brightest. The Orpheus rule is off: now you may look. Wind and water come forward in the sound. At ~2:24 everything holds still |
| | ~2:26 | She turns into the lost profile. A pearl earring catches the last of the sun in one glint, on the downbeat. A slight dolly zoom pulls the world back behind her as the music comes in at full strength. She holds the lost profile for about two bars |
| **5. Gone** | ~2:30 | On the next phrase the strongest gust of the song sweeps petals across her, and she's gone. Her scarf blows loose and catches on a willow branch |
| | 2:30–3:09 final chorus | Blue hour. The final chorus plays over the empty shore: mist rising off the water, the swing moving with no one on it, the crystals falling like snow and settling on the water as small lights that fade. The camera searches and finds only traces |
| **6. Afterglow** | 3:09–3:21 outro, then ~10 s | Night. Stars come out and double in the still water; a thin young moon sinks over the far shore. The camera stops, facing the water. The shore is empty, but in the reflection she stands at the water's edge. A breath, then the reflection is gone too. Crickets, then the end card ("Again") |

**Fixed and random.** The pass, the two almost-turns, the turn, the vanishing and the reflection always land where this table puts them. Where she stands for each glimpse (jetty, reeds, willow, rock), which side she passes from, and when the edge glimpses come change from play to play.

The camera travels along the east shore and looks out west across the water. She's almost always between the camera and the sun, so she's always backlit.

- **Water:** a big calm lake that reflects the sky, the hills, the sun's path, and her. Each gust front from the music runs across it as a band of dark ripples ("cat's paws"). Mist lies on it at blue hour, and at night it's still enough to double the stars.
- **Sky:** a sunset gradient, the sun's disc, clouds lit from below, then stars and a young crescent moon.
- **Far shore:** forested hills and a line of mountains in haze: the Meadow's panorama, re-lit for a sunset.
- **Near shore:** grass and wildflowers (the Meadow's plants), reeds at the waterline, a willow with a rope swing, a small wooden jetty, rocks, a low rise.
- **Air:** haze that glows toward the sun, pollen in the sunbeams, swallows in the golden hour, mist and a few fireflies at blue hour.

**One sunset, keyed to the song**

| Song time | Sun | Stage | Light |
|---|---|---|---|

| 0:42–2:12 | 14° → 2° | **Helen** | Golden hour, deepening to orange and rose: long shadows, the sun's path on the water |

| 2:30–3:09 | −1° → −7° | **Mary** | Blue hour: pink afterglow fading to blue, mist, the first star. Pale, distant, pure |
| 3:09 to the end | −8° → −16° | **Sophia** | Night: indigo, stars on still water, the young moon. Mystery, no answer |

## 5. Her

**Her look.** A long light dress (cream linen), long golden-blonde hair worn loose (against the sun it glows like a halo), and a long crimson scarf that streams in the wind. Slim and unhurried. She's never sharp and close at the same time.

**How she's built (procedural, no model files).**
- Body: a few smooth shapes (lathe and tube geometry). The dress is a cone of cloth that the vertex shader bends in the wind.
- Hair: 200–400 strands drawn as thin ribbons, simulated as spring chains (verlet) on the CPU. Backlit hair is her most important feature.
- Scarf: a small strip of cloth, also simulated.
- Head: a sculpted profile (brow, nose, lips, chin) that only ever shows as an outline against the light.

**Light.** Always backlit: a dark figure with a warm rim, hair and fabric glowing at their edges, haze in front of her by distance.

**Motion.** She breathes and slowly shifts her weight. Her hair, dress and scarf move in the same wind as the grass, so every gust front (one per bar) lifts her hair: her hair is the main way the music shows on her. She walks only when she's far away; the long dress hides her legs, so a simple walk reads well.

**Poses.**
- standing at the water, seen from behind
- walking away
- almost-turn 1: head about 25° round
- almost-turn 2: about 50°, the ear and the line of the cheek
- the turn: about 80°, the lost profile (cheek, lashes, the tip of the nose). Never further than a profile, never her face

**The glint.** A pearl earring catches the last sun at the turn: one bright point with a soft star flare, on the downbeat.

**Above eye level.** The song's title already says she's out of reach, so she often stands a little higher than the camera: on the rock, on the rise, at the end of the jetty.

**Her reflection** is rendered into the water. For the ending it's drawn without her.

## 6. Seeing her

- **Visibility** is one value the director sets for each moment. It drives everything that hides her: reeds and willow leaves in front of her, gusts of petals, depth of field (she's soft unless the director pulls focus to her for a beat), and haze.
- **The Orpheus rule.** Drag the view so she's in the centre, hold her there for about half a second, and she dissolves into petals and motes that the wind carries off. She turns up again later, somewhere else. Toward the edges of the view she stays fully visible. With nobody touching the controls, the camera does this on its own once, in verse 2. The rule is off for the breakdown and the turn.
- **Slow motion.** The world has its own clock, apart from the song's. It drops to about 20% for the breakdown and the turn (petals, hair, water and clouds slow down while the song plays on), and dips gently during her other clear glimpses.
- **Step-printing.** During glimpses her pose updates at 12 fps and leaves a faint smear: the stutter from Wong Kar-wai's films. Only on her; the world stays smooth.
- **Dolly zoom** at the turn: the camera moves in while the lens widens, so she stays the same size and the world behind her falls away (the *Vertigo* shot).

## 7. Traces, crystals, the world turning toward her

- **Traces:** the empty swing (still at the start, moving after she's gone), her crimson scarf caught on a willow branch, the grass flattened where she stood and slowly rising again, ripples spreading from the jetty.
- **Crystallization:** the sparkles on her outline grow with each glimpse, from a few at the first clear view to the most in the breakdown, and twinkle on the hi-hats. When she vanishes they fall like snow and settle on the water as small lights that fade.
- **Social proof:** flowers near her lean toward her, swallows circle above where she stands, petals drift her way.
- **Her colour:** crimson while she's there; white once she's a memory (the crystals, the mist, her moonlit reflection). That's the way Dante saw Beatrice: in crimson at nine, in white at eighteen.

## 8. Sound

- **Counterpoint, unison once:** the arc (§3) puts her clear views in the dips and her absence in the choruses. The only time music and picture peak together is the turn.
- **Prelude:** about 8 s of ambience before the song (phase 0).
- **Distance:** the song starts muffled through a low-pass filter, like a party across the water, and opens fully as she passes at 0:09. It closes a little whenever she dissolves.
- **Ambient bed, synthesized, so no extra files:** wind that follows the gust fronts, water lapping, cicadas by day, crickets at night, a rustle of fabric when she passes and when she turns. It comes forward in the dips and the breakdown.
- **Gentle beat reactions:** a gust front every bar (her hair, the grass, ripples on the water), light that breathes, a glide that speeds up with the music's energy. No pumping and no strobes.
- **No lyrics on screen.**
- **One limit:** opened straight from disk (file://), Chrome won't let Web Audio touch the song, so the filter only works on the website; from disk the song plays unfiltered. The ambient bed works everywhere.

## 9. The final image

- Painterly like the Meadow, with a film texture: grain, halation (a warm glow around bright light), bloom, a soft vignette and a slight frame weave.
- Light leaks at the turn and at the stage changes.
- Gentle depth of field with focus pulls.
- A softly faded palette, so her crimson is the strongest colour on screen.

## 10. Tech

- **One file:** `cleopatra/cleopatra.html`, like the other VJs: three.js 0.160 from jsDelivr, works from disk and on the website, loads `../vj-bridge.js` for the menu.
- **Copied and adapted from the Meadow:** helpers; the song analysis and its bake tool (`tools/bake.mjs`, so the page works from disk); the director's building blocks (entrances, bars, gust fronts); sky and cloud banks; the mountain panorama; lake shading; grass and flowers; drag to look; the owner panel; the quality manager; test hooks. The Meadow itself isn't changed.
- **New parts:**
  - her figure, with its hair, dress and scarf simulations;
  - the phase director (glimpses, almost-turns, the turn, the vanishing, the reflection) and its turn picker;
  - the Orpheus visibility;
  - the sunset rig, keyed to song time;
  - a half-resolution planar reflection for her, the jetty and the reeds (sky and hills reflect inside the background shader);
  - the crystals;
  - the world clock for slow motion, step-printing, the dolly zoom;
  - the traces (swing, scarf, flattened grass);
  - the sound bed, the prelude and the filter;
  - the end card.
- **Shader compile time:** loop counts bounded by a uniform from the start, `compileAsync`, measured early (what the Meadow taught).
- **Budget:** 60 fps on a mid-range laptop at 1080p (High), 30+ fps on phones (Low: fewer hair strands, no step-print smear, lower reflection and render resolution).
- **Reduced motion:** with the system setting on, there's no dolly zoom, no glint flare, no frame weave, and the glide is calmer.
- **Owner panel** on the <kbd>`</kbd> key: the song, a phase timeline with the turn marked, turn nudge (±1 beat), toggles (Orpheus rule, prelude, filter, step-printing, film look), quality, jump to a phase.
- **Your own song** (drag and drop, as in the Meadow): it's analysed and the phases come from its sections. The dips get glimpses, the loud sections get her absence, and the turn picker (§2) places the turn.
- **Test hooks** (`window.__cleo`): play at a song time, jump to a phase, force the turn, pose her, render one frame. Screenshots go through the vj-dev server.
- **Menu:** a new entry at the end of `VJS` in `index.html` (id and alias `cleopatra`, a crimson accent, blurb "She turns once, at sunset"), so it becomes the default VJ. Copy `3/` to `4/` for the short link vj.doslan.com/4.

## 12. Risks

| Risk | Plan |
|---|---|
| She looks stiff or cheap, and the spell breaks | Keep her mostly still, far away and backlit; hair and fabric carry the life. Check her as small thumbnails early in M3. If she doesn't convince, she shows less and the traces do more |
| The close pass looks like a glitch | Under a second, heavily out of focus, with the fabric rustle; timing tuned on screen |
| Slow, quiet images fight a 126 BPM pop song | The choruses stay alive (the glide, a gust every bar, swallows, petals); deep slow motion only in the breakdown and the turn |
| The turn misses the beat | It comes from the beat grid, can be nudged in the owner panel, and is baked into the page |
| The Orpheus rule annoys people | She dissolves only after about half a second in the centre, always comes back, and the rule is off for the turn |
| Hair and reflections are too slow on phones | Fewer strands at a lower simulation rate, half-resolution reflection, Low quality |
| A long shader compile | As on the Meadow: uniform loop bounds, async compile, measured from M1 |

## 14. As built (where it differs from the plan above)

- **Her dress** has long sleeves and a high neckline.
- **Her scarf** blows downwind onto a young willow inland of her spot, not onto the big willow, so the camera can follow it.
- **The swing** hangs from the big willow's low limb out over the water. In the "gone" part the camera finds it from the water's edge, against the lake.
- **Her reflection** at the end stands out on the water a few metres off the shore, seen from the water's edge. From the bank, the bank itself hid it.
- **The rush** in chorus 2 circles the whole lake and lands behind her at her spot.
- **Swallows** are drawn as small flapping sprites.
- **Speed:** grass farther than about 34 m and flowers farther than 30 m are dropped in the vertex shader, and the water's ripples come from a precomputed slope map. A frame costs about 1–3 ms at 720p on an RTX 2070.
- **Song analysis** is baked into the page with `tools/bake.mjs`, so it also works opened from disk. The distance filter on the song only works on the website.
